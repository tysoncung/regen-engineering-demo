## Scenario: the first address becomes the default

Given a customer registered with "ada@example.com"
When the customer adds address "1 First St"
Then the default address is "1 First St"
And the customer has 1 address

## Scenario: adding more addresses does not change the default

Given a customer registered with "ada@example.com"
And the customer has addresses "1 First St", "2 Second St"
When the customer adds address "3 Third St"
Then the default address is "1 First St"
And the customer has 3 addresses

## Scenario: deleting a non-default address leaves the default alone

Given a customer registered with "ada@example.com"
And the customer has addresses "1 First St", "2 Second St"
When the customer deletes address "2 Second St"
Then the default address is "1 First St"
And the customer has 1 address

## Scenario: deleting the default promotes the most recently used

Given a customer registered with "ada@example.com"
And the customer has addresses "1 First St", "2 Second St", "3 Third St"
And the customer used address "3 Third St"
When the customer deletes address "1 First St"
Then the default address is "3 Third St"
And the customer has 2 addresses

## Scenario: deleting the default with nothing used promotes the oldest

Given a customer registered with "ada@example.com"
And the customer has addresses "1 First St", "2 Second St", "3 Third St"
When the customer deletes address "1 First St"
Then the default address is "2 Second St"

## Scenario: deleting the last address leaves no default

Given a customer registered with "ada@example.com"
And the customer has addresses "1 First St"
When the customer deletes address "1 First St"
Then the customer has 0 addresses
And the customer has no default address
