> Raised by the reconciliation of commit `30d52e6` (T-025). The proposed rule is
> [BR-012](../rules/BR-012.md); the beliefs behind it are
> [ASM-002](../assumptions/ASM-002.md). **This issue is the finding.** The rule
> is a draft attached to it, not a decision, and merging BR-012 without
> resolving what follows would give this module two sources of truth about what
> `GET /customers/{id}/addresses` returns.

A hotfix capped `GET /customers/{id}/addresses` at the fifty most recently added
addresses. It changed implementation only, in both stacks, and touched no
knowledge. Three separate pieces of active knowledge disagree with it, and one
of the three disagreements is a live defect rather than a documentation gap.

## 1. It can silently break the BR-002 default invariant

This is the serious one.

[BR-002](../rules/BR-002.md) guarantees that a customer with at least one
address has **exactly one** default, and that the first address added becomes
the default and stays it until deleted. The hotfix keeps the *last* fifty of an
oldest-first list, so for any customer holding more than fifty addresses who has
never deleted their default, the default address is the one address guaranteed
to be dropped.

`GET /customers/{id}/addresses` is the only endpoint that exposes `isDefault`.
So the observable consequence is a 200 response containing fifty addresses, none
of which is the default, for a customer who does have one. That is not an edge
case reachable by unlucky ordering; it is what happens every time the cap
engages on a customer who never deleted their first address.

BR-002's promotion rule makes it worse rather than better. When the default is
deleted and nothing has been used, BR-002 promotes the **oldest** remaining
address, which is again exactly the address the cap discards.

Neither [CT-002](../contracts/CT-002.md) nor any other contract catches this,
because every scenario in the suite uses a handful of addresses. The full suite
is green against the hotfix: 24/24 across both stacks.

## 2. It cannot be true at the same time as BR-011

[BR-011](../rules/BR-011.md) caps a customer's address book at **twenty**. A
response cap of fifty on a store that holds at most twenty can never engage. On
the current knowledge, BR-012 describes unreachable code.

That leaves exactly two possibilities, and someone has to say which:

- **BR-011 is enforced everywhere.** Then no customer has thousands of
  addresses, the incident cannot have had the cause the commit message gives,
  and the correct outcome is to revert the hotfix and reopen the incident.
- **BR-011 is not enforced everywhere.** Then there is a path that creates
  addresses without checking the limit, BR-011 is not a true statement about the
  system, and [ASM-001](../assumptions/ASM-001.md) point 3 is wrong where it
  says "nothing in this system can currently produce a customer holding more
  than twenty". That is a larger finding than this hotfix.

A cap of fifty is incoherent in both worlds. In the first it is dead code; in
the second it is a second, looser limit sitting on top of a rule that claims to
be the limit, and the two numbers disagree about what "the address book" means.

## 3. The OpenAPI and the overview both say something else

[`api.openapi.yaml`](../api.openapi.yaml) is the authoritative wire contract
(REP-0002). It describes the 200 as "A bare JSON array" of the customer's
addresses, summarised as "List addresses, oldest first", with no cap, no
truncation, no pagination parameters and no total. `overview.md` repeats it:
"returns a **bare JSON array** of those objects, not an object wrapping them,
ordered oldest first."

Both are now false for a customer holding more than fifty addresses. Neither has
been edited, deliberately: they are the contract, and editing a contract to
agree with a hotfix is how a source of truth stops being one.

## What this issue is asking a human to decide

In order, because the later questions only matter if the earlier ones resolve a
particular way.

1. Can a customer hold more than twenty addresses today? Yes or no, with
   evidence. Everything else depends on this.
2. If no: revert `30d52e6` and reopen the incident, because the stated cause was
   not the cause. BR-012 and ASM-002 are then withdrawn rather than merged.
3. If yes: BR-011 is the thing that is wrong, and it should be fixed before any
   rule about response size is written. A response cap is not the right shape of
   answer to an unbounded address book.
4. Only if a cap on the response is still wanted after (1) to (3): decide
   whether truncation is acceptable at all, or whether the caller needs
   pagination or a total. If truncation is kept, BR-002 has to say what happens
   to the default, and the answer cannot be "it disappears".
5. Whatever is decided, `api.openapi.yaml` and `overview.md` change as part of
   the same delta, not afterwards.

## What was not done, and why

- **No contract was written**, although the `reconcile` skill asks for one when
  the behaviour is checkable. It is not checkable: BR-011 makes a fifty-first
  address unreachable through the documented interface, so no scenario can put
  the system into the state BR-012 describes. Reaching around the API to set up
  the state would have produced a green test for an incoherent rule.
- **No decision record was written.** The diff and the commit message contain no
  evidence that alternatives were weighed. Writing an ADR with a "rejected
  alternatives" section would have invented deliberation that did not happen.
- **Nothing was deleted or edited to make things tidy.** BR-002, BR-011,
  `api.openapi.yaml` and `overview.md` are untouched.
